Ordinals let people inscribe NFTs directly onto Bitcoin. No side chain, no extra database — the file lives on the sat. Satoshi Punks was one of Mongolia’s first Bitcoin Ordinals collections — 100 punks, inscribed between #36151 and #73597. At Numad Labs I owned brand, storytelling, and the NFT community from the first frame to the last mint. It sold out. Top sale $13,500. Eight BTC of volume.

Each punk is drawn on a 24-pixel grid: masks, halos, visors, pipes, and a few one-offs that became the faces of the account. The X page grew past 30,000 followers on the strength of those faces.

Pepepunks was the spin-off: 1,350 pieces on cursed and recursive inscriptions, rare sats, two cultures in one trait sheet. I handled the marketing, the KOL map, and the campaign frames that made “normal vs cursed” readable in a second. It sold out in two hours.

Graphic work in service of a drop: trait sheets, story frames, social kits, and a world people could join before they minted.
